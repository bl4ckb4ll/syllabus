# Electronic music — syllabus and course provenance

Targeted university-course sweep, 2026-08-31. This is a discovery set, not a claim to cover every university or every electronic-music syllabus.

The cluster began with Brown’s analog-synthesis teaching and the Buchla/ARP/Radigue/Oliveros/Ciani circle, then expanded to courses that materially overlap: electronic/electroacoustic music history, modular synthesis, computer music, women in electronic music, and sound practices.

## Brown University

Current 2026–27 bulletin material includes:

- **MUSC 1240N — Analog Practice**: modular and semi-modular analog synthesizers, explicitly including Brown’s rare **ARP 2500**.
- **MUSC 0200 — Computers and Music**: production, history, and theory of music technology, practical electronic-music work, and electronic-music literature/history.
- **MUSC 1240F — Circuit Bending and Hardware Hacking as Musical and Artistic Expression**.
- **MUSC 1240I — Building Musical Instruments**.
- **MUSC 1240S — Feminist Sonic Futures**.
- **MUSC 0221A — MEME Ensemble: The Rap Cypher & Freestyle Performance**, which explicitly applies Pauline Oliveros’s Deep Listening theory to hip-hop improvisation.

Kristina Warren’s Brown CV identifies her as designer/teacher of *Analog Practice*, *Electronic Music Aesthetics, Perception, Analysis*, *Composing with Ableton*, *Building Musical Instruments*, and *Studies in Electroacoustic Improvisation*.

Sources:
- https://bulletin.brown.edu/music/
- https://vivo.brown.edu/docs/k/kwarren1_cv.pdf
- https://vivo.brown.edu/display/twinkler

## Baruch College / CUNY — MSC 3032 Electronic Music

The current catalog describes a historical/practical electronic-music course spanning the nineteenth century to the present, including electronic/electroacoustic composition, studio production, DIY instrument-making, computer music, and analog/digital synthesis.

CUNY Academic Works hosts Ted Gordon’s 2022 **Zero Textbook Cost Syllabus for MSC 3032 (Electronic Music)**. Search-indexed syllabus text includes:

- Don Buchla and Charles MacDermed, “February 9th 1971”
- Éliane Radigue, “The Mysterious Power of the Infinitesimal”

## Ohio University — MUS 4130/5130: The History and Practice of Electronic Music

Mark Phillips’s Fall 2012 full HTML syllabus combines history, listening, terminology, technology, and hands-on lab work.

Books/readings include:
- [Joel Chadabe — *Electric Sound*](../../../Joel%20Chadabe%20%E2%80%93%20Electric%20Sound%20%E2%80%93%20The%20Past%20and%20Promise%20of%20Electronic%20Music.md)
- [Barry Schrader — *Introduction to Electro-Acoustic Music*](../../../Barry%20Schrader%20%E2%80%93%20Introduction%20to%20Electro-Acoustic%20Music.md)
- Robert Runstein, *Modern Recording Techniques* (chapter 2 suggested)

Relevant units include earliest electronic instruments and tape music; **pioneers of the analog synthesizer (Moog, Buchla, etc.)**; subtractive/additive synthesis; RCA Mark II; and later Csound, SuperCollider, FM, MIDI, granular synthesis, Max/MSP/Jitter, and Kyma.

Source:
- https://sites.ohio.edu/phillipm/M4130_5130_Syllabus.html

## University of Iowa — Composition: Electronic Media I

The Electronic Music Studios preserve historical syllabi. The Fall 1994 syllabus (Larry Fritts) introduces acoustics, audio theory, tape recording/manipulation, digital synthesis, MIDI sequencing, and electronic-music history.

Historical units include electronic music 1900–1948, tape studios of the 1950s, voltage-controlled synthesizers of the 1960s, and computer music. Assigned readings include [Paul Griffiths](../../../Paul%20Griffiths%20%E2%80%93%20A%20Guide%20to%20Electronic%20Music.md), H. H. Stuckenschmidt, Herbert Eimert, Curtis Roads, [Thom Holmes](../../../Thom%20Holmes%20%E2%80%93%20Electronic%20and%20Experimental%20Music%20%E2%80%93%20Technology%2C%20Music%2C%20and%20Culture.md), James Tenney, and Jim Aikin.

Sources:

- https://theremin.music.uiowa.edu/teaching.html

## Foothill College — MUS 11D: History of Electronic Music: Origins–1970

Current course outline, effective Fall 2026. It covers futurism, musique concrète, elektronische Musik, the New York School, Columbia–Princeton Electronic Music Center, tape composition, early synthesizers, early computer music, and the impact of race, ethnicity, and gender on electronic-music development.

The voltage-controlled-synthesizer unit explicitly names the **Moog synthesizer** and **Buchla synthesizer**. Representative text: [Thom Holmes — *Electronic and Experimental Music: Technology, Music, and Culture*](../../../Thom%20Holmes%20%E2%80%93%20Electronic%20and%20Experimental%20Music%20%E2%80%93%20Technology%2C%20Music%2C%20and%20Culture.md).

Sources:

- https://catalog.foothill.edu/course-outlines/MUS-11D/MUS-11D.pdf

## Wesleyan University — MUSC 225: Sound Systems and Chamber Electronics

Students create site-specific interpretations of fixed-media work by composers such as **Éliane Radigue**, Luc Ferrari, and Robert Ashley. Ensemble realizations may include John Cage’s *Cartridge Music* and David Tudor’s *Rainforest IV*. The technical side draws on **Moog and ARP synthesizers** plus SuperCollider.

Source:
- https://catalog.wesleyan.edu/courses/musc/

## Reed College — MUS 217: Women in Electronic Music

Survey + studio course explicitly centered on **Éliane Radigue, Pauline Oliveros, Maryanne Amacher, Wendy Carlos, Laurie Spiegel**, Björk, Laurie Anderson, Janet Cardiff, and others. Students analyze historical work and apply techniques in original synthesis, sampling, field-recording, beatmaking, songwriting, and storytelling projects.

Source:
- https://www.reed.edu/music/courses/

## Johns Hopkins / Peabody — PY.610.650: Pink Noise: Women Making Electronic Music

The seminar combines feminist critical frameworks, musical analysis, and listening practices. It uses Pauline Oliveros’s Deep Listening and Hildegard Westerkamp’s conscious-listening approach and says it is inspired and guided by [Tara Rodgers — *Pink Noises: Women on Electronic Music and Sound*](../../../Tara%20Rodgers%20%E2%80%93%20Pink%20Noises%20%E2%80%93%20Women%20on%20Electronic%20Music%20and%20Sound.md).

Named pioneers include **Daphne Oram, Pauline Oliveros, Wendy Carlos, Ruth Anderson, and Adrian Piper**.

Source:
- https://e-catalogue.jhu.edu/course-descriptions/musicology/

## Berklee College of Music

**ISEL-201 — Women in Electronic Music** includes pioneers **Suzanne Ciani** and Wendy Carlos and later artists including Jlin, TOKiMONSTA, Rachel K Collier, and Carolina Eyck.

**MLAN-340 — Sounding Oliveros: The Life and Work of Pauline Oliveros** studies Oliveros’s electronic and acoustic compositions, writings, listening philosophy, improvisation, pedagogy, and Deep Listening.

Sources:
- https://college.berklee.edu/courses/isel-201
- https://college.berklee.edu/courses/mlan-340

## School of the Art Institute of Chicago — analog sound courses

Whitney D. Johnson’s Fall 2026 teaching includes:

- **Analog Sound Studio**: analog recording, acoustics, and analog processing; named examples include **Ruth White, Suzanne Ciani, Éliane Radigue**, Laetitia Sonami, Arthur Russell, Laraaji, and Trevor Wishart.
- **Retro Sound: Ghost in the Machine**: vintage analog synthesis and tape using oscillators, function generators, synthesizers, reel-to-reel tape, envelopes, timbral composition, and tape reproduction without a DAW; named artists include **Éliane Radigue** and **Maryanne Amacher**.

Source:
- https://www.saic.edu/profiles/faculty/whitney-d-johnson

## NYU / Clive Davis Institute — REMU-UT 1024: Synthesis and Sound Design in Popular Music I: 1960–1983

Historical + hands-on synthesis course. The current bulletin says the class virtually emulates and deconstructs the **Moog Modular, Minimoog, ARP 2600, and Buchla 100**, then later synthesizers. Named artist casework includes **Suzanne Ciani** and **Wendy Carlos** among many others.

Source:
- https://bulletins.nyu.edu/courses/remu_ut/
