# Touch — Touch Radio listening archive

Touch is a London-based audiovisual label founded in 1981. Its **Touch Radio** project began in 2005 and became a large free online archive of radio art, field recording, electroacoustic composition, live performance, soundscape work, and experimental broadcasting. In 2011 the British Library added the complete Touch Radio archive then existing to its Sound Archive.

- Touch Radio: https://touchradio.org.uk/
- Touch: https://touch33.net/
- British Library archive note: https://touchradio.org.uk/touch_radio_at_the_british_library.html
- TouchPod: https://touchradio.org.uk/touchpod.html

This syllabus note links to the recordings and their documentation. Unlike PennSound, the Touch pages reviewed here do not state one blanket educational-reuse license for the whole archive, so audio should be treated as link-only unless an individual item says otherwise.

## Why this belongs in the electronic-music cluster

Touch is especially useful for the parts of electronic-music history that disappear if the syllabus is organized only around synthesizers and studio composition:

- field recording and acoustic ecology
- hydrophones, contact microphones, geophones, accelerometers, and ambisonics
- site-specific and environmental composition
- radio art and documentary collage
- sound installation
- musique concrète and recorded-object practices
- the boundary between unprocessed documentation and composed sound
- vinyl/tape playback as a compositional instrument
- environmental, architectural, and infrastructural sound

## Chris Watson — *The Galapagos Islands: an audio diary* (2005)

https://touchradio.org.uk/touch_radio_5_chris_watson.html

37:55. A field-recording diary made in the Galápagos, moving through several habitats and including underwater work with a four-channel hydrophone array. Good for introducing environmental recording as both documentation and composition.

## Chris Watson — *The Sound of Islay: a report from The Hebrides* (2007)

https://touchradio.org.uk/touch_radio_25_chris_watson.html

24:20. Wind, surf, vegetation, wetlands, and nocturnal animal sound from Islay. A concise example of location recording organized by time of day and habitat.

## Jacob Kirkegaard — *Eldfjall* live (2006)

https://touchradio.org.uk/touch_radio_12_jacob_kirkegaard.html

33:00. Icelandic geothermal and ice recordings captured with conventional microphones and accelerometers. Kirkegaard describes mixing the different recordings while leaving the source sounds otherwise unprocessed. Useful for contact vibration, geologic sound, and the difference between capture and transformation.

## Jana Winderen — Live at Ash 13 (2006)

https://touchradio.org.uk/touch_radio_16_jana_winderen.html

15:04. Built solely from hydrophone recordings made in Berlin, Lake Mjøsa, Halifax, and Oslo, with samples by Doug Quin. A compact entry point into underwater recording and inaccessible sonic environments.

## Jana Winderen — *Daybreak; Kochi (Kerala), India* (2017)

Available from the Touch Radio archive front page:
https://touchradio.org.uk/

38:03. A dawn recording made from 4:45 a.m. in Kochi. Useful for soundscape, duration, daily cycles, and listening as an observational practice.

## z'ev — untitled (2005)

https://touchradio.org.uk/touch_radio_4_zev.html

50:03. Described by z'ev as “binary-acoustics”; intended for close headphone listening. A useful contrast with documentary field recording: the ear is still the main instrument, but the sound world is deliberately constructed.

## Philip Jeck — Touch Radio 6 (2005)

https://philipjeck.com/category/radio/

A 47:55 program assembling live material, earlier recordings, sampler pieces, and excerpts from *Instructions for Survival*. Jeck's broader practice makes this a natural bridge into turntables, vinyl decay, sampling, memory, and media archaeology.

## Steve Roden — *9-Sided Room* (2007)

https://touchradio.org.uk/touch_radio_24_steve_roden.html

27:00. Roden layered nine sides of his own vinyl releases, including a childhood recording made in a coin-operated phono booth. An unusually clear syllabus example of records themselves becoming the source material for a new piece.

## Hildur Guðnadóttir / Charles Matthews — *Nacre* (2009)

https://touchradio.org.uk/touch_radio_42_charles_matthews_and_hildur_gudnadottir.html

46:39. A composition for church organ and light organ, composed by Hildur Guðnadóttir and performed by Guðnadóttir and Charles Matthews, with an installation by Elín Hansdóttir. Good for electroacoustic work that crosses composition, installation, architecture, and light.

## Richard Crow — *Imaginary Hospital Radio* (2010)

https://touchradio.org.uk/touch_radio_52_richard_crow.html

41:34. A radiophonic collage that mimics and subverts hospital radio using visceral, surgical, clinical, and institutional sound. Useful for sound art, montage, the body, and institutional environments.

## Aino Tytti — *Hellissandur Mast [GRD 7970]* (2013)

https://touchradio.org.uk/touch_radio_96_aino_tytti.html

16:49. Contact-microphone recordings from the Hellissandur longwave mast in Iceland. Wind and thermal expansion excite the steel structure, producing shifting resonances; at one point the microphones also pick up the longwave broadcast. Excellent for structural vibration, radio infrastructure, and unintended music.

## Bethan Kellough — Krafla Geothermal Power Station / Hverir (2015)

https://touchradio.org.uk/touch_radio_112_bethan_parkes.html

27:28. Unprocessed recordings from a geothermal power station, mud pools, steam vents, pipes, and surrounding landscape, made with a SoundField microphone and contact microphones. Useful for industrial/environmental boundaries and spatial recording.

## Félix Blume — *A La Orilla (On the edge)* (2017)

https://touchradio.org.uk/

45:01. Field recording at the edge of the Amazon rainforest in southern Venezuela, emphasizing the border between human settlement and a dense nonhuman sound environment.

## Geneva Skeen & Sarah Rara — Live in Los Angeles (2017)

https://touchradio.org.uk/

37:03. Skeen's *Pop Song* responds temporally to Yann Novak's drone installation; Rara's *Separating the Air* works at the boundary between speech and music. Useful for a course that wants to connect installation, voice, computation, and contemporary political form.

## Aino Tytti — *Slicing the Troposphere* (2019)

https://touchradio.org.uk/

18:36. Wind-turbine recordings made with ambisonic and stereo microphones, geophones, and contact microphones. Touch provides stereo, binaural, and ambisonic forms. This is a particularly good technical example because one piece can be discussed simultaneously as infrastructure study, field recording, and spatial-audio practice.

## Stuart Bowditch — *Noon bells in Sicily* (2019)

https://touchradio.org.uk/

1:00:00. Noon recordings from several Sicilian churches, edited into a sequence that preserves surrounding context. Useful for repeated-observation methods: same time of day, changing location.

## Ian Wellman — *Between the City and the Forest* (2019)

https://touchradio.org.uk/

12:31. Field recordings from Los Angeles, the Mojave, and the Sierra Nevada: insects, birds, frogs, electromagnetic textures, city sound, and web-SDR material. A concise example of environmental and electromagnetic listening in one composition.

## Machinefabriek — *Moving Noises 2015* (released on Touch Radio 2019)

https://touchradio.org.uk/

22:34. Improvised live performance using an analog tone generator, radio, contact microphone, and pedals. Useful for showing how an electronic-music practice can be both minimal in equipment and unstable in result.

## Patrick Francke — *Iceland Miniatures for Inga Martel* (2021)

https://touchradio.org.uk/

32:49. A reflective field-recording work explicitly concerned with the gap between the tourist's imagined Iceland and lived/ecological reality. Touch also links an ambisonic mix.

## Adie Mueller & Armin Lorenz Gerold — *Another Goodbye* (2021)

https://touchradio.org.uk/

51:54. Spoken word, writing, performance, composition, and soundscape combined into an audio work about death, grief, and separation during the pandemic. Useful as a reminder that radiophonic composition can be narrative and documentary rather than purely abstract.

## Touch 2024 in Review — Robin the Fog (2024)

https://touchradio.org.uk/

59:59. A recent overview of Touch-associated work, including Richard Chartier, Claire M Singer & Philip Jeck, KMRU, Fennesz, Rosy Parlane, and others. Useful as a contemporary endpoint after the historical examples above.

## Possible syllabus units

### Field recording as composition

Chris Watson, Jana Winderen, Félix Blume, Bethan Kellough.

### Contact sound and hidden vibration

Jacob Kirkegaard, Aino Tytti, Bethan Kellough.

### Infrastructure as musical material

Aino Tytti's radio mast and wind turbines; geothermal machinery at Krafla.

### Recorded media as instrument

Philip Jeck and Steve Roden.

### Spatial audio

Aino Tytti's ambisonic work and Touch's binaural / multichannel presentation.

### Radio art and sound narrative

Richard Crow; Adie Mueller & Armin Lorenz Gerold.

### Sound, architecture, and installation

Hildur Guðnadóttir / Charles Matthews; later Touch Radio installation works.
