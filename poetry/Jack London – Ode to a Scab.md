# Ode to a Scab

**Traditionally attributed to Jack London (1876–1916); attribution uncertain.**

> “After God had finished the rattlesnake, the toad, and the vampire, He had some awful substance left with which he made a scab.”

## Full text

The complete commonly circulated text is reproduced in the U.S. Supreme Court opinion *Old Dominion Branch No. 496, National Association of Letter Carriers v. Austin*, 418 U.S. 264 (1974):

https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/US/418/418.US.264.72-1180.html

The Court calls it “a well-known piece of trade union literature, generally attributed to author Jack London.”

## Early circulation and attribution

A substantially identical passage appeared as an “American definition of a scab” in the *Industrial Worker* of April 10, 1913:

https://www.marxists.org/history/usa/pubs/industrialworker/iw/v5n03-w211-apr-10-1913-IW.pdf

A February 21, 1913 report in New Zealand’s *Maoriland Worker* also quotes the passage without naming London:

https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/MW19130221.2.8?query=glue

A modern etymological note argues that anonymous versions circulated before London’s name became attached to the piece:

https://www.wordorigins.org/big-list-entries/scab

Because the text was in print by 1913, it is in the public domain in the United States.
