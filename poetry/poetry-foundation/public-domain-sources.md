# Public-domain sources and mirror candidates

## U.S. cutoff

The U.S. Copyright Office states that **all works published in the United States before January 1, 1931 are in the public domain**.

Source:

- Copyright Office Circular 15A: https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ15a.pdf
- Copyright Office overview: https://copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/

For *Poetry: A Magazine of Verse*, founded in Chicago in 1912, this makes the issues published through 1930 a large U.S. public-domain corpus.

This does **not** mean every old-looking text currently displayed on poetryfoundation.org should be copied. A modern translation, edition, annotation, photograph, recording, or editorial introduction can have its own copyright. The safe workflow is to identify the work through the catalog, then obtain the actual text from an independent public-domain edition.

## Confirmed source: Volume I, October 1912–March 1913

### Project Gutenberg

- Ebook page: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/43224
- HTML edition: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/43224/pg43224-images.html

Project Gutenberg identifies this edition as **public domain in the USA**. Its transcription was produced from HathiTrust images.

This is currently the easiest clean-text seed for an actual local mirror.

### Wikimedia Commons scan

- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Poetry,_a_magazine_of_verse,_Volume_1_(October_1912-March_1913).djvu

The Commons record identifies the scan as public domain in the United States because of its publication date.

### Wikisource transcription / page layer

- Example page from Volume I: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Poetry,_a_magazine_of_verse,_Volume_1_(October_1912-March_1913).djvu/89

Wikisource is useful when we want page-image provenance and proofread status rather than treating OCR as unquestionable text.

## What Volume I contains

The first volume alone includes work by or material involving poets such as:

- Harriet Monroe
- William Butler Yeats
- Ezra Pound
- H.D.
- Richard Aldington
- Vachel Lindsay
- Rabindranath Tagore
- George Sterling
- Alice Corbin
- Witter Bynner
- John Reed

The point of mirroring the early magazine is not only to collect isolated famous poems. It preserves the poems beside editorials, reviews, notes, arguments about Imagism, advertisements, books received, and the developing institutional history of modern poetry.

## Mirror queue

### Tier 1 — mirror in full from independent public-domain sources

- *Poetry: A Magazine of Verse*, Volume I (Oct. 1912–Mar. 1913), from Project Gutenberg / Commons / Wikisource.
- Subsequent *Poetry* issues and volumes published through December 1930, as clean scans or transcriptions are located and their provenance recorded.
- Individual poems independently confirmed as public domain, preferably from a scan or established public-domain edition.

### Tier 2 — metadata + link + our own indexing

- Copyrighted poems on Poetry Foundation.
- Modern Poetry magazine issues.
- Modern translations of public-domain originals.
- Modern collections and anthologies.

Keep:

- author
- title
- date
- original source / book
- Foundation discovery URL where useful
- our own topic/emotion tags
- public-domain/copyright status

Do not keep the full copyrighted text.

### Tier 3 — summarize and cite

- Poetry Foundation biographies
- essays and criticism
- poem guides
- editorial introductions
- historical essays

These are valuable research sources, but the local contribution should be our own summary plus bibliography and links.

### Tier 4 — link unless separately licensed

- audio
- video
- photographs
- artwork
- modern magazine scans

## Provenance rule for mirrored text

Every locally mirrored work should say where its bytes/text came from. Suggested fields:

```text
title:
author:
first_publication:
local_source:
source_url:
source_scan_url:
public_domain_basis:
proofread_status:
topics:
emotions:
```

Do not write “public domain” merely because an author died long ago. Record the basis: publication date, source edition, or explicit public-domain statement from a reliable repository.

## Do not scrape the Foundation

The Poetry Foundation's Terms of Service prohibit automated spiders, crawlers, scrapers, harvesters, and indexers:

- https://www.poetryfoundation.org/terms-of-use

So the Foundation should serve as a human-facing discovery map and citation target. Public-domain mirroring should proceed from Project Gutenberg, Wikisource, Wikimedia Commons, HathiTrust, Internet Archive, original scans, and similar repositories instead.
