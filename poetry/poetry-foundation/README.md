# Poetry Foundation — discovery index and public-domain corpus

The Poetry Foundation is useful to this syllabus less as a website to copy than as a large map of poetry: author, period, form, subject, occasion, geography, and especially human experience and emotion.

This directory records that indexing idea while keeping the syllabus independent of the Foundation's website.

## Reuse rule

1. **Do not spider or scrape poetryfoundation.org.** The Poetry Foundation's current Terms of Service expressly prohibit bots, crawlers, indexers, spiders, scrapers, harvesters, and similar automated collection.
2. **Modern copyrighted poems:** keep bibliographic metadata, our own notes, topic links, and a link to the Foundation or another legitimate source. Do not copy the poem merely because it is free to read in a browser.
3. **Public-domain poems and old issues:** these can be mirrored when we obtain them from an independent public-domain source such as Project Gutenberg, Wikisource, Wikimedia Commons, HathiTrust, Internet Archive, or a scan of the original publication. Do not use the Foundation site as the extraction source.
4. **Foundation biographies, essays, introductions, photographs, recordings, and editorial prose:** link and summarize in our own words unless a separate license clearly permits copying.
5. **Translations and editions:** check the translation/editorial copyright separately even when the underlying poem is ancient or otherwise public domain.

Sources:

- Poetry Foundation Terms of Service: https://www.poetryfoundation.org/terms-of-use
- U.S. Copyright Office, duration of copyright: https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ15a.pdf

As of 2026, the U.S. Copyright Office states that all works published in the United States before January 1, 1931 are in the public domain. That makes the 1912–1930 run of *Poetry: A Magazine of Verse* a particularly valuable mirror target, provided the text is taken from a lawful public-domain source rather than scraped from the Poetry Foundation.

## Indexing model worth preserving

The Foundation's current browse structure separates:

- Subjects
- Occasions
- Holidays
- Poetic Terms
- Emotions
- School / Period
- Geography
- Children's Subjects
- Themes

That is a useful model for Syllabus because it lets a reader enter poetry through a human problem or experience — grief, guilt, love, anger, fear, family, work, death, war — rather than requiring the reader to begin with an author name or literary period.
