# Topics, themes, and emotions

The strongest thing to borrow from the Poetry Foundation is not its page layout but its many-to-many classification of poems by human experience.

A poem should be able to appear under several entrances at once. Grief can coexist with guilt; love with disappointment; war with family; death with memory; work with class; faith with doubt. This is more useful for a general reader than a single shelf such as “Victorian” or “Modernist.”

Foundation provenance:

- https://www.poetryfoundation.org/topics
- https://www.poetryfoundation.org/topics-themes
- https://www.poetryfoundation.org/categories/mobile-moods

The Foundation currently describes its topic browser in broad groups including **Subjects, Occasions, Holidays, Poetic Terms, Emotions, School/Period, Geography, and Children's Subjects**, with a separate Themes layer.

## Emotional / experiential cross-index

This is a Syllabus-controlled cross-index seeded from labels and groupings visible in the Foundation catalog. It is intentionally many-to-many and is not claimed to be a complete copy of the Foundation taxonomy.

Foundation collection:

That collection itself subdivides loss by situations such as remembering a parent, lost love, death of a child, and regret/depression. The useful idea is the subdivision, not copying the Foundation's editorial prose.

## Public-domain examples already useful for cross-indexing

These examples show why a poem should have several topic links rather than one canonical shelf.

- Alfred Tennyson — *Tithonus*: the Foundation currently associates it with Blame & Guilt, Disappointment, Grief, and Melancholy & Despair, among other categories. The underlying nineteenth-century poem is public domain; use a public-domain edition for any local text.
  - Foundation discovery page: https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/45389/tithonus
- William Shakespeare — *Song: “Come away, come away, death”*: the Foundation associates it with Love, Death & Dying, Mourning, Blame & Guilt, Disappointment, Grief, and Melancholy & Despair. The Shakespeare text is public domain; again, source local text from a public-domain edition rather than scraping the Foundation.
  - Foundation discovery page: https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/50654/song-come-away-come-away-death-

## Syllabus indexing rule

For every poem we add, prefer a small metadata block or adjacent index links containing as many applicable entrances as useful:

- author
- title
- date / first publication
- literary period
- geography / language
- form
- subjects
- occasions
- emotions
- relationships
- social / historical context
- source of the text
- copyright / public-domain status

The emotion and experience fields should never be exclusive. A poem about a parent's death can belong simultaneously to grief, guilt, family, memory, death, faith, anger, and consolation.
