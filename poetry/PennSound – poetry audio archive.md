# PennSound — poetry audio archive

PennSound is the University of Pennsylvania's large archive of recorded poetry, readings, talks, interviews, radio programs, and sound poetry. It is unusually useful for a syllabus because PennSound's stated model is free, downloadable audio cleared for **noncommercial and educational use**; copyright remains with the authors or estates.

- Main archive: https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/
- Authors index: https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/authors.php
- Manifesto / reuse model: https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/manifesto.php
- Singles: https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/singles/
- Classics: https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/linking-page/classics.php

This file links to recordings rather than copying audio into the repository. PennSound explicitly encourages teachers to add its MP3s to online syllabi and allows noncommercial educational recollection, but individual pages still carry author/estate copyright notices.

## Historical voices — Speech Lab Recordings, 1931–early 1940s

https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/Speech-Lab-Recordings.php

One of the earliest systematic attempts to record and distribute poets' voices. The recordings were made on aluminum discs at Columbia University by W. Cabell Greet and George W. Hibbitt and circulated in part as the National Council of Teachers of English / Columbia **Contemporary Poets Series**.

The surviving collection includes recordings by:

- Vachel Lindsay
- Harriet Monroe
- James Weldon Johnson
- Gertrude Stein
- Edgar Lee Masters
- William Carlos Williams
- Mark Van Doren
- Edna St. Vincent Millay
- T. S. Eliot
- Robert Frost
- W. H. Auden
- Archibald MacLeish
- Alfred Kreymborg
- John Gould Fletcher
- Robert Tristram Coffin
- Conrad Aiken
- George Russell (AE)
- Carl Sandburg
- John Hall Wheelock

### Gertrude Stein

https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/Stein.php

The page includes Stein's January 30, 1935 Speech Lab session: *A Description of the Fifteenth of November: A Portrait of T. S. Eliot*, *A Valentine to Sherwood Anderson*, *Christian Bérard*, excerpts from *The Making of Americans*, *Matisse*, and *If I Told Him: A Completed Portrait of Picasso*.

### William Carlos Williams

https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/Williams-WC.html.save

The archive includes his 1942 Contemporary Poets Series reading, including *The Red Wheelbarrow*, *Tract*, *The Defective Record*, *To a Poor Old Woman*, *To Elsie*, *The Wind Increases*, and *Classic Scene*, plus later Library of Congress material.

## Amiri Baraka / LeRoi Jones

https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/Baraka.php

A substantial archive for the relation between poetry, Black music, politics, performance, and criticism. Material includes readings, interviews, *Wise, Why's, Y's*, discussion of nation language and appropriation, *Funk Lore*, *Bee-Bop (for Dizzy Gillespie)*, *Reflections (for Thelonious Monk)*, *Sounding*, and historical recordings going back to the 1960s.

## Robert Creeley

https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/Creeley.php

Large author archive spanning readings, interviews, radio, oral history, and discussions of Charles Olson. Useful for Black Mountain poetry and for hearing how Creeley's lineation and pauses work in performance.

Selected-works page:
https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/Creeley-Selected.php

## Lyn Hejinian

https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/Hejinian.php

Readings, talks, interviews, and later memorial material. Especially useful alongside *My Life*, Language writing, collective poetics, and discussions of the sentence and prose poem.

## Adrienne Rich

https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/Rich.php

Includes readings from *Dark Fields of the Republic*, *Fox*, and *The School Among the Ruins*, along with material on translation and the relation between poetry and public life.

## S Press — sound poetry and experimental voice

https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/S-Press.php

The S Press archive is a strong bridge from written poetry into composition and sound art. It includes John Cage's 1972 recording of *Mureau*, made by writing-through Henry David Thoreau's journals, along with work by Bernard Heidsieck and other experimental writers and performers.

## Tape Poems

https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/Tape-Poems.php

A compact syllabus unit on poetry made specifically for tape. Contributors include Joseph Ceravolo, Dan Graham, Bernadette Mayer, John Perrault, Anne Waldman, Lewis Warsh, and Hannah Weiner. Weiner's pieces explicitly work with motion, duration, recorded playback, helium/krypton, and the sonic behavior of objects.

## The World Record — Poetry Project performances, 1969–1980

https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/World-Record.php

Selections from the St. Mark's Poetry Project tape archive, including Anselm Hollo, Charles Reznikoff, John Wieners, Joe Brainard, and others. Useful for the New York poetry scene as an actual performance culture rather than only a printed canon.

## Public Access Poetry, 1977–1978

- First set: https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/PAP.php
- Second set: https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/PAP-2.php

Preserved public-access cable television readings connected to the Poetry Project. The surviving programs include Bernadette Mayer, Lewis Warsh, Eileen Myles, Alice Notley, Charles Bernstein, Hannah Weiner, and many other downtown New York writers and performers.

## PennSonido — Spanish-language recordings

https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/PennSonido.php

Spanish-language recordings and interviews, including Víctor Hernández Cruz, Odi Gonzales, Soleida Ríos, Reina María Rodríguez, Mónica de la Torre, Cecilia Vicuña, Raúl Zurita, and the bilingual Radio Reading Project. Useful for a syllabus that does not want English-language poetry to appear as the entire field.

## Radio Readings Project

https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/RRP.php

Readings and interviews designed as a trans-American radio project. The surviving programs include Robert Creeley and Susan Howe, among others, and preserve extended commentary as well as readings.

## Berkson Tapes

https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/Berkson-Collection.php

A wide-ranging collection from the poetry-performance network around San Francisco and New York. It includes Ted Berrigan, Robin Blaser, Joe Brainard, Basil Bunting reading *Briggflatts*, and John Cage talks/readings including *Mesostic IV* and *Overpopulation and Art*.

## Classics

https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/linking-page/classics.php

Useful when the poet cannot be heard in an authorial recording. PennSound collects later readings of canonical work by poets including Blake, Chaucer, Dickinson, Whitman, Yeats, Auden, and others. This can support comparison between silent reading, interpretation by another reader, and surviving author recordings where available.

## Possible syllabus uses

- Read a poem silently, then hear the poet read it.
- Compare several historical performances of the same work.
- Teach line break, rhythm, stress, pause, accent, and breath as audible features rather than typographic abstractions.
- Put poetry next to radio, tape, public-access television, performance art, jazz, and electronic sound.
- Use historical recordings as primary documents in literary history.
- Use PennSonido and the Radio Readings Project to make translation and multilingual performance part of the core syllabus rather than an appendix.
