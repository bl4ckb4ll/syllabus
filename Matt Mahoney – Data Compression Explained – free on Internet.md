# Matt Mahoney — *Data Compression Explained*

Free online book on the theory and practice of data compression.

- **Author:** Matt Mahoney
- **Current cited edition:** copyright 2010–2012, Dell, Inc.; last update 15 April 2013
- **Official free text:** https://mattmahoney.net/dc/dce.html
- **Earlier PDF:** *Data Compression Explained*, copyright 2010 Ocarina Networks; last update 26 February 2010: https://nishi.dreamhosters.com/u/dce2010-02-26.pdf

## What it covers

Mahoney presents compression as a combination of modeling and coding, with lossless and lossy methods treated in the same broad framework. Topics include information theory and algorithmic complexity, benchmarks, Huffman and arithmetic coding, PPM and other variable-order models, PAQ/ZPAQ-style context mixing, LZ-family methods, transforms, image/audio/video compression, and the relationship between prediction, modeling, compression, and artificial intelligence.

The intended reader is someone who wants to understand how compression works or write compression software; Mahoney states that some prior programming and mathematics are expected.

## Free-copy / redistribution status

This is not merely a web page that happens to be publicly accessible. The book itself contains an explicit redistribution permission.

The current Dell-copyright version permits copying and distribution provided that the license travels with the material, the material is not modified, and no fee or other consideration is required for the copies or for works incorporating the material. The February 2010 Ocarina-copyright version contains substantially the same permission.

That makes an unchanged repository mirror legally plausible under the stated conditions. This entry currently links to the upstream copies rather than duplicating the book; any future mirror should preserve the book and its license unchanged.

## Seen in university teaching

A University of Wrocław data-compression course page lists **Matt Mahoney — *Data Compression Explained*** among its course links/resources. The page separately identifies Sayood, Drozdek, Wayner, Salomon/Motta, Salomon, and Pu as books/handbooks, so this evidence establishes Mahoney's book as a course resource but does **not** by itself establish that a particular chapter was assigned.
