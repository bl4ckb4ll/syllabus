University Course Description

This course introduces students to rhetorical concepts and audience-centered approaches to writing including composing processes, language conventions and style, and critical analysis and engagement with written texts and other forms of communication.

Course Format

Discussion-based and collaborative course engagement are significant means of learning in this course. Modality is either in-person or asynchronous online, and full engagement in course learning is heavily emphasized in both modalities. Students are expected to read, discuss, interact, participate, reflect, draft and revise, respond to feedback, and otherwise engage as instructed in a sequenced set of assignments that lead to completion of each of four projects. Projects are scaffolded to build learning throughout the course, and require engagement in the writing processes and learning activities in order to meet student learning outcomes. 

Student Learning Outcomes

  • Students will apply rhetorical knowledge to communicate for a range of audiences and purposes.
  • Students will employ critical thinking to analyze forms of communication.
  • Students will engage in writing processes that involve drafting, revising, and reflecting.

Course Objectives

In ENC 1101, students will learn:

  • To develop rhetorical knowledge for effective communication to a range of audiences and for various purposes.
  • To understand how knowledge of writing concepts and practices can be applied effectively across various writing situations.
  • To analyze, interpret, and convey conclusions about a range of texts and representations of meaning.
  • To engage in critical thinking and reflective practices that help develop analytical and problem-solving abilities and foster meta-cognition.
  • To compose in different genres using knowledge of concepts and practices of writing and drawing on analysis of other texts as examples of writing strategies appropriate to context.
  • To effectively communicate ideas and present information clearly and concisely across contexts, in written, oral, and digital forms.
  • To understand and engage in composing as a recursive process that involves drafting, revising, and reflecting across multiple iterations.

How to Succeed in this Course

Students will be evaluated on the knowledge and practices of writing they develop, on the drafts and final compositions produced, on specific course engagement activities, and on weekly collaborative work, readings, and a range of low-stakes writing-to-learn activities in class or assigned as homework. 


Attending and engaging in class and with course work is important in the study of writing as the subject matter of this course. As a discussion-based course, this involves not just submitting assignments but learning about writing effectively and appropriately to each situation. Much of the learning is completed during class or engagement activity and cannot be replicated otherwise. Success in each project in this course depends greatly on attendance and engagement in class.


Online courses have similar expectations. Although students do not physically attend, they must engage with the course materials and collaborate with others in a sequence designed for engaged learning. This means staying on pace throughout the course and engaging in weekly discussions and activities that build toward subsequent weeks and projects.


This is not a self-paced course.

#GEA 1 – General Education Assessment Assignment

The Final Analysis Essay as listed above under Project 4 will assess student learning. In this project, students will write an essay both reflecting on and analyzing their knowledge about writing as they look back over their work in the course. Through a series of prompted reflection responses and drawing on their own work as evidence, students will theorize about and articulate the knowledge they have developed about rhetorical concepts, communicating responsibly, understanding information presented across contexts, for various audiences and purposes, and utilizing appropriate tools and rhetorical moves. In a clear and cohesive essay, students will analyze their writing knowledge and connect what they know to the writing they have studied that demonstrates understanding of rhetorical concepts across contexts, audiences, and purposes.

Instructor Feedback Policy & Grade Dissemination

Feedback

Instructors provide formative, actionable feedback on writing assignments or drafts to help students move successfully through the sequenced learning of the semester. Course engagement activities vary in design and expected feedback; all provide a measure of learning and an opportunity for students to build their knowledge toward the development of each project.


Instructors will provide more substantial feedback on selected individual student work throughout the semester, such as project drafts. Feedback will be provided collectively on selected activities or project work. Students are responsible for revisions, using feedback, self-assessment activities, and reflection on knowledge and process to make rhetorical decisions about their own written work. 


Instructors will provide feedback in a timely manner to allow students time to consider suggestions and make revisions to project work. Instructors provide feedback in Canvas, or via in-class presentation or Canvas Announcement for collective discussion. 


Feedback is an important part of the writing process, and it is expected that students consider feedback carefully and ask questions as necessary regarding feedback received. Whether or not to incorporate feedback into writing or revision is ultimately the writer's decision.

This is primarily a discussion-based, writing intensive course which requires attendance, collaboration, and engagement in class in order to learn. Netiquette Guidelines

  • Act professionally in the way you communicate. Treat your instructors and peers with respect, the same way you would do in a face-to-face environment. Respect other people’s ideas and be constructive when explaining your views about points you may not agree with. 
  • Be sensitive. Be respectful and sensitive when sharing your ideas and opinions. There will be people in your class with different linguistic backgrounds, political and religious beliefs or other general differences. 
  • Proofread and check spelling. Doing this before sending an email or posting a thread on a discussion board will allow you to make sure your message is clear and thoughtful. Avoid the use of all capital letters, it can be perceived as if you are shouting, and it is more difficult to read. 
  • Keep your communications focused and stay on topic. Complete your ideas before changing the subject. By keeping the message on focus you allow the readers to easily get your idea or answers they are looking for. 
  • Be clear with your message. Avoid using humor or sarcasm. Since people can’t see your expressions or hear your tone of voice, meaning can be misinterpreted.
