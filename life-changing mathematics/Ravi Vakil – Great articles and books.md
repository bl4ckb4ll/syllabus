# Ravi Vakil — “Great articles and books”

Status: curated-source entry rather than an individual mathematical paper.

## Links

- Ravi Vakil, **Great articles and books**: https://math.stanford.edu/~vakil/greatwriting.html
- Ravi Vakil’s homepage: https://math.stanford.edu/~vakil/

## Thanks and credit

Vakil says this page grew out of a spring 2008 algebraic-geometry lunch about examples of unusually good mathematical writing. The suggestions are intentionally presented as a sampling of individual tastes rather than as a canonical ranking, and the contributors are not named item by item. Thanks belong both to Vakil for preserving the list and to the mathematicians who supplied examples and reasons.

## Why this is here

The selection rule is unusually close to the purpose of this directory. The page explicitly distinguishes “great writing” from merely important theorems or books that happen to be the standard reference. The motivating reasons include work that changed a reader’s life, helped inspire a mathematical career, or feels indispensable to a field.

That makes the page useful in two ways: as a bibliography of strong mathematical exposition and as evidence about what mathematicians themselves describe as formative reading.

## Summary

The page records a deliberately non-canonical list of articles and books proposed as examples of great mathematical writing. Vakil refuses to turn the exercise into a fixed rubric: the point is to look at examples and decide what makes them work. He also warns that the list reflects the tastes of the people who contributed to it and is correspondingly weighted toward algebraic geometry.

For this repository, the page should be treated as a source of leads, not as a list to mirror wholesale. Particularly compelling items can receive their own entries here with their own provenance, summaries, lawful links, and reasons for inclusion.
