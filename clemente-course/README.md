# Clemente Course in the Humanities

This directory reconstructs Earl Shorris's original 1995–96 Clemente Course in the Humanities and keeps later Clemente reading lists separate from the first-course evidence.

There was not one immutable national Clemente syllabus. The first New York course is unusually well documented, but later sites developed their own syllabi. Do not silently turn later Clemente readings into claims about the 1995–96 course.

## Evidence labels

- **ATTESTED ORIGINAL** — explicitly identified in Shorris's contemporary account of the 1995–96 course.
- **RETROSPECTIVE** — identified by Shorris or an original teacher later, but not clearly attested in the contemporary account.
- **CONTEXT ONLY** — discussed or placed on the course timeline, but not established as assigned reading.
- **LATER CLEMENTE** — used or recommended by a later Clemente site; not evidence about 1995–96.
- **UNRECOVERED** — a component is known to have existed, but the exact text or assignment has not yet been identified.

## Original shape

The original course followed the Hutchins-era University of Chicago discussion-course model. The five rotating subjects were:

1. moral and political philosophy
2. art history
3. logic
4. poetry/literature
5. American history

Shorris's recruitment pitch also promised **rhetoric**. No separate first-year rhetoric syllabus has yet been recovered, so rhetoric is not silently merged into logic or poetry here.

The course ran roughly October through May, with two 90-minute evening meetings each week and a two-week Christmas break. Subjects rotated rather than running in long blocks, both to integrate the disciplines and to reduce the damage from unavoidable absences.
