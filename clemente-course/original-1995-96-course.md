# Original Clemente Course, 1995–96

## What can actually be reconstructed

The first Clemente Course met at the Roberto Clemente Family Guidance Center in lower Manhattan. Shorris modeled it on the Hutchins-era University of Chicago: serious primary texts, discussion, papers and tests, and no watered-down "remedial" humanities track.

Thirty students were admitted. Sixteen completed the first course and fourteen received Bard College credit.

Primary contemporary account:
https://harpers.org/archive/1997/09/ii-as-a-weapon-in-the-hands-of-the-restless-poor/

### Faculty

- **Earl Shorris** — moral/political philosophy and, after the first history instructor withdrew, American history.
- **Charles Simmons** — poetry/literature. Novelist; former assistant editor of *The New York Times Book Review*; had taught at Columbia.
- **Grace Glueck** — art history. Art critic and reporter for *The New York Times*.
- **Timothy Koranda** — logic. Had graduate training at MIT and had published in mathematical logic.
- **Thomas Wallace** — began the American-history section, then withdrew; Shorris took it over.

## Moral and political philosophy

### ATTESTED ORIGINAL

The sequence Shorris described before the course began was:

1. **Plato, *Republic*, Allegory of the Cave** — added at Viniece "Niecie" Walker's insistence and made the first assignment.
2. **Plato, *Apology***.
3. **Plato, *Crito*** — a selection.
4. **Plato, *Phaedo*** — a few pages, so students would know what happened to Socrates.
5. **Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics***.
6. **Thucydides, *History of the Peloponnesian War*, Pericles' Funeral Oration** — used to connect ethics to politics.
7. **Sophocles, *Antigone*** — read simultaneously as drama, moral philosophy, and political philosophy.

Thirty copies of Bernard Knox's *The Norton Book of Classical Literature* supplied the philosophy texts except *Republic* and *Nicomachean Ethics*. This identifies the anthology actually present in the classroom, though it does not by itself prove that every work in the anthology was assigned.

Public-domain alternative texts, useful for reading but not necessarily the translations used in Clemente:

- Plato index, MIT Internet Classics Archive: https://classics.mit.edu/Browse/browse-Plato.html
- Plato, *Apology*: https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/apology.html
- Plato, *Crito*: https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/crito.html
- Plato, *Phaedo*: https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/phaedo.html
- Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics*: https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.html

### Method

Shorris said there would be **no philosophy lectures**. He used Socratic/maieutic dialogue: the work of the class was to question, answer, distinguish, and revise rather than receive a survey lecture.

### RETROSPECTIVE

In a later conversation Shorris also recalled **Hume** as part of the philosophy course. The exact Hume work has not yet been identified, and Hume is not named in the detailed 1997 description of the planned first-course sequence. Keep this as retrospective evidence, not as a silently certain 1995–96 assignment.

## Poetry / literature

### ATTESTED ORIGINAL or contemporary description

Charles Simmons did not rely on a single textbook. Poems were handed out and read closely in class. The recoverable sequence includes:

- very short poems, jokes, puns, and poems treated initially as puzzles;
- **A. E. Housman** — exact poems not yet recovered;
- **D. H. Lawrence** — a poem about America; exact title not yet recovered;
- **Maya Angelou** — exact poem(s) not yet recovered;
- **William Blake, "The Little Black Boy"**;
- **Langston Hughes** — a poem describing a Harlem park; exact title not yet recovered;
- **Latin poetry** — Simmons had said the course would end there; exact authors/poems not yet recovered.

### UNRECOVERED

A complete packet of Simmons's poetry handouts has not yet been located. Do not substitute a later anthology and call it the original packet.

## American history

### ATTESTED ORIGINAL plan

Shorris described the history course as history **through documents**. The planned line was:

1. **Magna Carta**;
2. **John Locke, *Two Treatises of Government*, Second Treatise**;
3. **Declaration of Independence**;
4. additional American political/historical documents;
5. documents through the **Civil War**.

When Shorris later took over the history teaching, **Gordon S. Wood, *The Radicalism of the American Revolution*** was discussed. The surviving evidence located so far does not establish that students were assigned the whole book, so it should not be promoted to "required reading" without better evidence.

### UNRECOVERED

The complete document packet from Magna Carta through the Civil War has not yet been recovered.

## Logic

### ATTESTED ORIGINAL

Timothy Koranda taught the logic section. Contemporary accounts establish that formal/mathematical logic and truth-table style reasoning were part of the course, and describe students working symbolic problems at the blackboard.

### RETROSPECTIVE

Koranda later recalled that Shorris asked him to devise a mathematics class using mathematical logic to teach abstraction. He said he began with **Aristotle** and worked toward **propositional logic** and **first-order predicate logic**.

Source: Timothy Koranda, letter to *Harper's*, November 2013:
https://harpers.org/archive/2013/11/letters-814/

### UNRECOVERED

The original handouts, exact Aristotle selection, problem sets, truth-table exercises, and examination questions have not yet been recovered.

## Art history

### ATTESTED ORIGINAL

Grace Glueck planned a chronological course beginning with **cave painting** and extending to the **late twentieth century**. Recoverable material includes:

- cave art / Lascaux as an opening reference;
- ancient Egyptian art;
- a Metropolitan Museum of Art visit centered in part on the **Temple of Dendur** and Egyptian collection;
- work in the Met's Rockefeller Wing, including material from **Mali**, **Benin**, and the **Pacific Islands**;
- slides and direct looking at objects rather than a purely textual art-history course.

### UNRECOVERED

No complete original slide list, image list, textbook assignment, or museum worksheet has yet been recovered.

## Cross-disciplinary work

### ATTESTED ORIGINAL final paper

A final paper asked students to use *Antigone* together with what they had learned about **Socrates, Sojourner Truth, Henry David Thoreau, and Simmons's poetry** to state and defend their own view of Antigone's decision.

The exact Sojourner Truth and Thoreau texts used in preparing that paper have not yet been recovered.

## Orientation timeline — CONTEXT ONLY

Shorris drew a large historical/cultural timeline to orient the class. It ran through roughly:

- Neolithic myth
- *Epic of Gilgamesh*
- Old Testament
- Greeks
- Confucius
- New Testament
- Qur'an
- *Epic of Son-Jara*
- Nahuatl and Maya poetry
- European contact with the Americas

This is **not** evidence that complete versions of all these works were assigned. It was contextual framing for the intellectual chronology of the course.

## Rhetoric

Shorris told recruits that the course offered "philosophy, poetry, art history, logic, rhetoric, and American history." The recoverable first-course teaching rotation, however, is philosophy, art, logic, poetry, and history. A distinct rhetoric syllabus has not been recovered.
